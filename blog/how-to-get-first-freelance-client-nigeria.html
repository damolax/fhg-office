How to Find Your First Freelance Client in Nigeria: Portfolio to Proposal

You can finish a course and still have no customers. That doesn't mean your training was wasted; it means learning a skill and selling a service are different jobs. Here's a practical, respectful way to move from samples to client conversations.

By Oyekunle Olalekan Musodiq• October 20, 2026

Step one: stop selling “skills”; offer a result

When a buyer contacts a freelancer, they usually want a problem solved. A restaurant wants an updated menu and Instagram graphics. A consultant wants a landing page that explains their services. A small shop wants a simple email list and welcome message.

Describe the result in language that a business owner understands. “I can build your booking page and make sure customers can contact you on WhatsApp” is more concrete than “I do tech.”

Turn practice into a trustworthy portfolio

A portfolio is evidence that you can produce useful work. You don't need to pretend you've served ten paying clients. Create realistic sample projects, label them as concepts, explain the business problem and show your approach.

Your skillPortfolio exampleWhat to explain
Web designSample website for a fictional cleaning companyNavigation, contact flow, mobile layout
Graphic designFive campaign graphics for a fictional cafeColours, typography, target audience
Email marketingThree-email onboarding seriesAudience, purpose and sequence
ResearchSample prospects database using public dataSelection criteria, data quality and source handling

If you helped a real person for free, get their permission before sharing the project or testimonial. Never claim somebody else's Fiverr dashboard or payment as your earnings.

Where can Nigerians find potential clients?

Different channels work for different services. Use whichever matches your ability and the rules of that channel.

ChannelHow to use it responsiblyWhat to prepare
FiverrPublish a specific Gig with accurate deliverablesOriginal samples and clear packages
UpworkRespond to relevant job posts with tailored proposalsProfile, examples and a brief project plan
ReferralsTell contacts exactly which business problem you solveShort, shareable portfolio link
Direct outreachIdentify businesses with a genuinely relevant needOne specific observation and a respectful offer
Local businessesDiscuss a useful improvement, with no pressureExample solution and honest price range

A simple personalised outreach message

Hello, I noticed your business shares great photos but customers may have to search through different posts to find your services and opening hours. I create simple mobile-friendly service pages. I made a short sample of how that could look. Would you like to see it?

This works because it identifies a relevant problem, says what you do, and asks permission rather than demanding a sale. Research responsibly and respect any refusal or opt-out. Don't invent compliments or mass-send the same message to hundreds of unrelated contacts.

How should a first project be priced and delivered?

Work out your time, tools, revisions, and delivery expenses before making an offer. The numbers below are only budget-planning illustrations—not market averages or guaranteed project prices.

Illustrative projectAgreed scopeExample budgetImportant boundary
Event flyerOne flyer, two rounds of changes₦15,000Extra concepts cost more
Basic landing pageOne page, contact link, mobile check₦75,000Domain and hosting are separate
Email welcome sequenceThree messages plus basic testing₦45,000Platform subscription not included

Before starting, agree on payment terms, client materials, approvals and what happens if the scope changes. Use the contract and payment protections offered by your platform when working through marketplaces.

Keep follow-up professional

If a prospect doesn't reply, one polite follow-up can be enough to clarify whether the project is relevant. If they decline, thank them and move on. Learn from responses: are customers confused by your service, pricing or portfolio? Improve those things rather than increasing message volume.

A good first client is not just an order total—it is someone whose problem you solve well, under fair terms, with a deliverable both parties understand.
