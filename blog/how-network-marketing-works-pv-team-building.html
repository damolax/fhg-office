How Network Marketing Works: Products, PV, Teams and FHG's Skills-First Approach

If someone says “network marketing is a business of team building,” what does that really mean? Let's explain the products, payments, PV and team structure using simple numbers—and then show why FHG encourages people to develop digital skills and active income first.

Start with a real product, not a recruitment pitch

Imagine a company that manufactures wellness products. It needs people to introduce products to customers, explain their uses accurately and help customers make informed purchases. As a distributor, you may buy products at a distributor price and resell them at the permitted retail price.

For example, suppose a product costs you ₦20,000 and the approved retail price is ₦25,000. If you genuinely sell it to a customer at that price, your gross retail profit is ₦5,000—before transport, delivery, marketing, payment charges, taxes or any unsold stock. These figures are illustrative only, not actual prices for an FHG partner-company product.

This is the first idea: there can be income from genuine product sales. Buying stock is not the same as selling it, and spending ₦20,000 does not mean you have earned anything.

Why would the company also pay a commission?

A distributor is an independent participant in the company's sales and marketing network, subject to the contract. A company may pay eligible distributors an additional commission according to its published compensation plan. That commission is different from the profit you might earn by reselling a product.

Commission is not automatically a fixed share of every sale. Depending on the plan, it may depend on eligible retail demand, verified customer sales, personal or group volume, rank, qualification periods, returns and other rules. Before relying on it, read the partner company's compensation plan and income disclosure carefully.

Two different things: Retail profit comes from what you charge a customer minus the cost of goods and expenses. Commission is a separate payment determined by the company's rules. Neither should be confused with revenue from merely buying inventory.

What is PV in network marketing?

PV usually means Personal Volume or Point Volume: an internal point value a company uses to measure certain qualifying product activity. Different companies define PV differently; some count eligible personal purchases, some count customer orders or other qualifying volume. Team or group volume is often calculated separately. A naira spent is not necessarily one PV, and 250 PV is not automatically ₦250 or ₦250,000.

Think of PV as a measuring unit in the reward plan, not money. The company's written rules determine what generates points, whose points qualify, and which points can contribute to your team total.

How building a team can increase your group's PV

You could try to generate a large volume alone, but the principle of team building is to work with other independent distributors. Some may be siblings, cousins, friends or people you meet through legitimate business networking. They should join voluntarily, understand the costs and be interested in actual customers—not pressure or income promises.

Let's use your example: you record 250 PV. Seven other participants each generate 250 PV of qualifying activity. If the compensation plan allows all eight contributions to count toward the same group volume, you would have 2,000 group-volume points.

2,000 PV is eight times 250 PV in volume, not necessarily eight times the commission. As the group grows, different company rules may allow different bonus levels. But percentages, rank qualifications, caps, customer-purchase requirements and expenses all affect the actual amount.

You might hear claims that 2,000 PV pays “28 times” what 250 PV pays, or that 4,000 PV pays “more than 80 times.” Those cannot be established from PV mathematics alone. Without the partner company's verified pay table and realistic earnings evidence, they should not be presented as a likely result or a guarantee.

What building a responsible distributor team actually involves

A good team is more than names in a group chat. It means training people to understand the products, talk to real customers honestly, keep useful records and help newer distributors learn the business. A responsible leader doesn't need to pressure people to buy more stock than they want or can sell.

How FHG tries to make the starting point easier

At Faitheroic Global Team (FHG), the philosophy described on this website is learn a digital skill first, understand how to monetize that skill, work toward active client income, then consider network marketing. The purpose is to reduce the pressure of depending only on product sales while you're still learning how to earn.

For example, someone might begin with free training in email marketing, website design or graphic design; practise and make a portfolio; and look for freelancing clients through platforms such as Fiverr or Upwork. When they have built useful experience and some active income, they can choose whether to explore the network marketing business and the obligations that come with it.

The next person they mentor can follow the same skills-first approach. Instead of asking someone to sign up and immediately buy products, the emphasis becomes learning, serving clients, financial readiness and responsible team support. None of this guarantees freelancing income or distributor commissions.

For a broader explanation of earnings-claim and inventory risks, see the FTC's business guidance on multi-level marketing. Company-specific qualification rules always take precedence over made-up examples.

The big idea: learn, earn, then build with people

The appeal of network marketing is shared effort. Instead of treating every target as one person's job, a group of independent people can serve customers, learn from each other and, where permitted by the plan, combine eligible volume. FHG's approach is to help people build marketable digital skills first, so their starting point is not entirely dependent on moving products.
